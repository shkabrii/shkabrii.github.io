Biography

I am a PhD student in the Research Group Data Mining and Machine Learning at the University of Vienna, supervised by Prof. Claudia Plant and co-advised by Prof. Martin Pawelczyk. Before that, I completed my M.Sc. in Computer Science at the University of Vienna, graduating with distinction in 2025. I am interested in understanding how neural networks organize information in their embedding space, and in developing principled methods to make learned representations more robust, fair, safe, and trustworthy.

Broadly, my interests span representation learning, adversarial robustness, fairness in machine learning, and AI safety. I enjoy research that combines empirical work with theoretical analysis, and I am drawn to questions where careful reasoning about geometry and information can lead to simple, principled solutions.

Research Areas

Representation Learning

Understanding how deep networks structure information in their embeddings, from clustering to reinforcement learning.

Fairness

Building models whose decisions do not rely on sensitive attributes such as race, age, or gender, with formal notions of fairness backed by theoretical guarantees.

Adversarial Robustness

Studying how and why neural networks fail under perturbations and adversarial attacks, and developing principled ways to make them more resilient.

AI Safety

Making powerful models reliable and aligned with human intent, with an interest in unlearning, deception detection, and interpretability.
